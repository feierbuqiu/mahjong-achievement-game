# Current status: kernel-certificate research

**The current target is a saved, closed Lean theorem for the actual original
empty-state game, followed by cold standard-kernel replay of its dependencies.**
The work now concerns persistent proof certificates and compression. The
actual opening theorem remains **`NOT_VERIFIED`**.

The earlier V16 native/compiled-checker campaign is historical context. Its
13.0256-hour known-component estimate, including 25% margin and excluding six
production costs, does not estimate the current proof workload. The previous
expectation of an imminent sub-20-hour finish is superseded. **No reliable
20-hour or 24-hour end-to-end bound has been established.** The legacy whole-table
scan remains stopped; bounded proof experiments have run separately.

## Recorded progress

- **V17:** 13 new generic proof modules connect finite checked input, actual
  source/target identity, cross-layer induction and the root decoder to the
  original-game conclusion. A 145-module fresh kernel replay succeeded. The
  actual full-input acceptance equations remain uninstantiated.
- **V18:** an actual-data, saved-proof pilot closed strategies for two explicit
  32-tile P roots and one 32-tile N root using an 11-node certificate. Separate
  checker statements cover 56 distinct captured rows and two overlapping scalar
  cases. The final frozen closure cold replay succeeded in 176.562 seconds.
- **V19:** compression, supplied numeric P/N/layout checks, local-metadata proofs
  and generic local certificate/layer bridges have made component progress.
  Their sample scopes and timings do not establish a complete original-game
  certificate, global SAFE coverage or whole-task performance.

The [current source/evidence update](../research/2026-10-18-kernel/README.md)
distinguishes these results, publishes selected completed snapshots and lists
remaining source/distribution dependencies. In particular, the V19 public
subset is not a complete portable cold-replay distribution.

The September exhaustive computation continues to report a second-player win
under its stated implementation and input dependencies. Its full reverse
replay shares rules and indexing. The old compiled-checker total of 303,054,865
rows in layers 32–34 retains its original compiler/runtime and IO boundary;
those rows have not automatically become closed kernel certificates here.

## What remains

The actual certificate must instantiate the finite premises, bind the exact
rules, data and required successors, preserve P/all-response and N/witness
obligations, and close the actual root. Its proof data must survive saving and
cold replay: erased runtime results, file hashes and JSON PASS flags cannot
stand in for a proof term. General composition theorems are available; their
actual whole-instance application is unfinished.

A sufficient sparse or compressed empty-game strategy need not verify every
historical table row. Any separate claim to certify the entire SAFE table has
the stronger full-coverage obligation. Neither claim is currently complete.
Construction, metadata/layout work, persistence, assembly, dependency loading,
memory/disk use and final replay all belong in a future practical cost assessment.

Independent arguments, deeper verification, alternative certificate designs,
corrections and well-scoped experiments remain welcome. This publication starts
no new computation and does not release the legacy whole-table hold.
